# Talking with the Family: 13 and over, server side, capped; listening stays on the device

Status: proposed 2026-10-21 in the `family-helpers` change (owner decisions of 2026-10-21, D14 and D15). Built in its step 3, after react-typescript phase 2; accepted when the owner gives the go (task 3.6). The conversation's shape was settled on 2026-10-24 (design D18) and is folded in below; what a turn sends did not change. Builds on ADR 0006 (under 13 posts nothing), ADR 0008 (the Grade answer stays on the device) and ADR 0014 (the server voice and its `VoiceLedger`).

The Nikhilam Family helpers are scripted: fixed lines plus the Skill's own data, said in the page, nothing sent. Two later layers would change what the site sends, so they are decided here.

**Talking with the Family (an AI conversation).** Opt-in, and only for a Player whose saved answers say 13 or over: an Age answer of 13 or over, or, with no Age answer, a Grade answer of grade 9 or above or Grown-up; an Age answer under 13 always rules it out. Nothing for any other Player reaches the endpoint. Each turn the browser sends our Worker the Skill id, the numbers of the question on screen and the words the Player typed or said for that turn, with a short-lived `family`-scope session ticket the Worker signs after a Turnstile check (the bot protection below; the conversation route accepts that ticket alone, never a `voice` ticket or a Firebase ID token); never the Nickname, a Player id, the Age or Grade answer or earlier visits. The Worker adds the Family's system prompt (their voices, and "a hint, the method or an example on other numbers, never the answer") and sends the model provider only that prompt and the turn. No turn's words, reply or ticket are logged or kept; the only thing kept is the bot protection's hourly count per address below. Spend is capped on the server at $5 a day and $50 a month at list price, in its own limit inside the same `VoiceLedger` Durable Object; at the cap, on a refusal, a slow answer or offline the Family fall back to scripted help without an error.

**The shape of a turn (owner, 2026-10-24, design D18).** Bolt joins Shunya, Nava and Eka and answers every why or how question. "The words for that turn" are this Visit's words: the Player's messages and the replies so far in the open Visit, which belongs to one question; nothing from an earlier Visit or question. One call per turn to Claude Haiku 4.5, from our own Worker route, with the reply capped at about 300 tokens, returns at most two short `{speaker, line}` replies and may ask for four actions (`showExample(a, b)` on numbers other than the question's, `pointAt(part)`, `showSutra()`, `react(mood)`), each checked by the app; none takes or shows the answer, and none changes a setting or moves on. A reply that hints at the question uses one of a fixed number of clues; at zero the speakers explain the method in general; general talk about the method or the Sutra is free. Replies are text only at first. Safety: fixed instructions keep the talk on math and the Mode, steer back from anything else, never ask for or repeat personal details and never take another role; the browser strips email addresses, phone numbers and long numbers that are not the question's before sending; the Worker replaces with a scripted line any reply that contains the answer or leaves the Family's and Bolt's voices; a note under the chat box says "Don't share your name or where you live." Bot protection: a Cloudflare Turnstile check once per browser session before the first message, and about 20 turns an hour per connection address, counted in the same `VoiceLedger` Durable Object under a keyed hash whose key changes daily and deleted when the hour ends. Turnstile never loads for a Player whose Age answer is under 13. The same check and limit apply to the server voice endpoint, which amends ADR 0014's access rule (a `voice` ticket now needs the Turnstile check).

**Listening.** Pro gets push-to-talk; any Player may turn on a listening mode with a visible mic badge that stops when the tab is hidden or after 2 minutes of silence. Speech recognition must run on the device (the browser's on-device mode); where it cannot, the listening mode is not offered, because a browser's default recognition sends audio to the browser maker. It only looks for help phrases, and a match is a help signal and nothing else. No audio, transcript or "a phrase was heard" is ever stored or sent. Push-to-talk for a 13 and over Player who has turned on the conversation sends the words of that turn (not the audio) as above.

## Considered Options

- **Scripted help only, forever:** free, offline and safe, but it cannot answer "why do we add one?" in the Player's own words. It stays the core and the fallback.
- **AI for every Player:** a child's own words are personal information under COPPA once an operator collects them, and the site is directed to children; without verifiable parental consent (ADR 0006) under-13 Players get scripted help only.
- **Call the model from the browser:** the key would ship to every visitor.
- **A self-asserted age sent with each turn:** it proves nothing and sends an age-like field (rejected in ADR 0006 for the same reason); the gate is in the client, on the device's answers, like World boards.
- **Always-on listening, or cloud recognition:** a microphone open on a children's site, and audio leaving the device; rejected.
- **CopilotKit (a chat framework with a hosted runtime), weighed 2026-10-24:** rejected. Measured: 293 KB gzipped on the client at its minimum and 732 KB with its provider, near the whole first-load budget of 758,499 bytes; a Worker of about 1 MB gzipped that needs `nodejs_compat`. By default it sends the full history and all app context each turn, keeps threads in an in-memory store readable without auth, sends telemetry on every turn and sets `max_tokens` to 64,000, each against what this ADR allows to be sent or kept; its onboarding command line tool is proprietary and links the project to its cloud. Our own small endpoint instead; it may later follow AG-UI's event shapes without the library.
- **A larger model or a long reply:** more cost per turn for short replies to a child's question; Claude Haiku 4.5 with about 300 reply tokens keeps a turn near $0.004, about 1,200 turns in the $5 day.
- **No bot check, or a check on every message:** without one, a script could spend the day's cap in minutes; on every message, a Player waits each turn. Once per browser session plus the per-address limit sits between.
- **A separate ledger for the conversation:** two places to keep caps consistent; one Durable Object with two limits is simpler and already strongly consistent.

## Consequences

- The privacy policy, the parent note and `cloud/README.md` say what a conversation turn sends, to whom, for 13 and over only, before it is switched on; the lawyer review of ADR 0006 covers it.
- Worst-case spend is bounded on the server ($5 a day, $50 a month) on top of the server voice's own caps; the provider's budget alerts stay the outer stop.
- Cloudflare Turnstile becomes a third party (in `THIRD_PARTIES` in `build.py` and the privacy policy with the task that adds it, before it runs); it never loads for a Player whose Age answer is under 13, and for any other Player only when they first talk with the Family or first need a new Server line in a browser session.
- A Player under 13 gets no new Server lines once the server voice needs Turnstile: they hear Server lines already on the voice host, the clips or the device's voice (react-typescript design, "Turnstile before the server voice"; the owner confirms in task 3.6).
- The per-address count is the one thing the server keeps for a while: a keyed hash, not the address, rotated daily and deleted within the hour; the privacy policy says so.
- An off switch on the Worker turns the conversation off without a deploy of the game, and the Family go back to scripted help.
- Reversing "never for under 13" would need verifiable parental consent and a new privacy notice, so it is treated as hard to reverse.
