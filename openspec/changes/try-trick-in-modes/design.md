# Design

## Context

The Mode picker (`modePicker()` in `js/app.js`) had one primary Try a trick button in its hero (`#tryBtn`). It set Challenger as a Mode guess (`fx.setMode('challenger', { guess: true })`, `modeGuess` on the Player record), started the day, sent `math/try/start` and opened `firstStarHref()`, Bolt's suggested Star (`#/star/from9/1`), with `starView.introFor` set so `runStar(..., { intro: true })` drew the worked example card of `try-trick-teaches` (`starIntro()`, `teach()`). Explorer has no Stars, so an Explorer could not take that path at all, and a Pro got a Challenger Star.

The owner's words (2026-10-21): "The try a trick should be inside the 3 levels and it should teach or aligned to some novel vedic math sutra that is applicable." The three levels are the Modes (Explorer, Challenger, Pro). The owner asked for no questions; each decision below was made from the repo's plans and is recorded here.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- A Try a trick that belongs to each Mode, reachable from that Mode's card on the picker and from that Mode's Home.
- Each one teaches one Trick under the Sutra it really comes from, with the Sutra named, said (Say it) and explained in the Mode's words, then lets the Player try three.
- Honest math and honest attribution: the Trick and its Sutra agree in `js/vedic.js` (`sutraId`) and `js/sutras.js` (`tricks`), and the copy credits the 1965 book and never the Vedas.
- Keyboard, screen reader, Calm and Pause motion as for `try-trick-teaches`; nothing new spoken.

**Non-Goals:**
- Placement, the Hook or the grade question. How they fit is in decision 9.
- A Star for Explorer, or a new Trick. All three Tricks already exist with their animators and Katas.
- The landing page at `/` (its Mode cards already open Home in a Mode, where the Try a trick card is).

## Decisions

**1. Which Sutra for each Mode, and why it is honest and novel.** Each is a Trick the app already teaches, filed under that Sutra in both data files, from Bharati Krishna Tirtha's 1965 book.

| Mode | Trick (`js/vedic.js`) | Sutra (`js/sutras.js`) | Worked example | Questions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explorer | `by11`, "The 11 sandwich" | s3 Ūrdhva-Tiryagbhyām, vertically and crosswise | 23 × 11: split into 2 and 3, 2 + 3 = 5 in the middle, 253 | 34, 26, 41 (× 11): tap the middle digit 7, 8, 5 |
| Challenger | `sq5`, squares ending in 5 | s1 Ekādhikena Pūrveṇa, by one more than the one before | 35²: 3 × 4 = 12, then 25: 1225 | 25², 45², 65² = 625, 2025, 4225 |
| Pro | `near-base`, multiply near 100 | s2 Nikhilaṃ Navataścaramaṃ Daśataḥ, all from 9 and the last from 10 | 97 × 96: distances 3 and 4, 97 − 4 = 93, 3 × 4 = 12: 9312 | 98 × 97, 94 × 96, 103 × 106 = 9506, 9024, 10918 |

- **Explorer, honest:** `js/vedic.js` files `by11` as "Ūrdhva Tiryagbhyām (special case)" and `js/sutras.js` lists `by11` under s3. The deep dive's proof, (10a + b)(10c + d) = 100ac + 10(ad + bc) + bd, with c = d = 1 gives 100a + 10(a + b) + b: the vertical steps keep the outer digits and the crosswise step is the sum in the middle. So the line "The middle is the across part. Add the two digits." is the sutra, not a decoration. **Novel and age-right:** a 4 to 7 year old only adds two small digits (every sum is under 10, so nothing carries) and gets a three-digit times-11 answer, shown as a sandwich picture. Rejected: casting out nines (Guṇitasamuccayaḥ, `check9`), too abstract for 4 to 7; "one more" counting under Ekādhikena, which the book does not teach that way (a misattribution); make ten under Nikhilam, which is the plain trick the owner asked to avoid.
- **Challenger, honest:** `sq5` is filed under s1, and the deep dive's proof is (10a + 5)² = 100 × a(a + 1) + 25: "one more than the one before" is exactly the a + 1. **Novel:** squaring 65 in one breath is the classic surprise of the book's first chapter, and it is not the subtract-from-100 trick every site starts with. Rejected: keeping `from9` (the old trial, the plain trick).
- **Pro, honest:** `near-base` is filed under s2; the sutra is how each distance from the base is found (97: 9 − 9 = 0, 10 − 7 = 3, so 03), which the card says in one line. **Novel:** 97 × 96 in one line, with the distances multiplied instead of the numbers, and the third question above 100 (103 × 106) shows the same rule adding. Rejected: Ūrdhva-Tiryagbhyām for Pro (2-digit times 2-digit). It is a fine Pro trick, but Explorer's 11 sandwich already uses that Sutra, and one Sutra per Mode makes three different first impressions.
- The Sutra meaning shown is `js/sutras.js`'s own (the owner-checked wording), except Explorer's, which says it in Explorer words ("Its name means straight down and across.").

**2. Where Try a trick sits.** Under each Mode card on the picker, as a full-width button directly below the card (`.mode-opt` holds the card and its button; a button cannot nest inside the card's button). Visible text "Try a trick" with an arrow, accessible name "Try a trick as <Mode>" (the visible words lead the name, WCAG 2.5.3). All three are primary buttons: they are three equal choices, one per card. On Home, a Try a trick card (`.try-card`, a plain `md-btn`, so each Home keeps exactly one primary action as "Three doors first" requires) sits after the three doors in Challenger and Pro and after the eight picture tiles in Explorer, until that Mode's Try a trick is finished on this device. It is not in the Sky home (an opt-in Home for Players who have played) or under More ways to play. Rejected: a Try a trick door among the three doors (it would push Play this Star or the Arena out of the first screen and change "Three doors first"); a 9th Explorer picture tile (the spec keeps eight).

**3. The single landing button and the Mode guess go.** The hero keeps only the headline, the line "One trick, two minutes, no sign-up." (still true: each card's Try a trick is one trick in about two minutes) and the Nikhilam Family. Its glow and the nine's nod toward the button go with the button. The heading becomes "Pick who is playing" (no "Or"). A card's Try a trick is a pick: `fx.setMode(mode)` (which sends `math/mode/<mode>` as a card does) and `gm.startDay()`. Nothing sets a Mode guess any more, so `setMode()` and `setPlayerMode()` lose their `guess` option. A Player record that already holds `modeGuess: true` keeps its "our guess for a first try" note until a pick clears it (`modeGuessed()` still reads it), so no one's Home changes silently.

**4. The route and the module.** `#/try` opens the Try a trick of the Player's Mode (`tryView()` in `js/app.js`); with no Mode the picker shows first, as for any route. `js/try.js` holds the three as data (`TRY`), pure helpers (`tryFor`, `sutraOf`, `choicesFor`, `finished`) that `test/try.test.mjs` checks without a page, and `runTry(root, { mode, signal })`, which draws three phases on child signals of the view's signal through `js/life.js` (`test/lifecycle-scan.test.mjs`). Challenger and Pro steps are the Trick's own `steps()` (the same lines as its animator and the Star replay); Explorer's three steps are written for Explorer (short sentences, at most 10 words, as the `age-bands` plan's Early band wants). A Try a trick is not a full-screen shell: it is a short page with the header, so Back is the browser's and the crumbs'.

**5. Teach, then try, then the next step.** Card: tag, title, lede, the Sutra block (the Trick page's `.mq-sutra` with its Say it button and Respelling), the why line, "Worked example: ...", the steps all at once (`.star-steps--still`), one start button (Explorer "My turn", others "Your turn") and "Then 3 questions like it. From Bharati Krishna Tirtha's 1965 book." Questions: Challenger and Pro type into a numeric box and Check (one try: right shows the answer and +10 Sparks; a miss shows the answer and the Trick's steps on those numbers); Explorer taps one of three big buttons for the middle digit (the sum and its two neighbours, as `age-bands` plans 3 choices for Early): a first miss turns that button off and says "Not quite. Try again!", a second shows the sum and the whole answer. Each result waits for a Next button: nothing advances on a timer. End: "You did it!" (Explorer) or "Trick learned!" (2 or 3 right) or "Good start!", the count, the Sparks, a line naming the Sutra, then Challenger and Pro: Play Star 1 of the same Trick (`#/star/sq5/1`, `#/star/near-base/1`) and Back home; Explorer: Back home and Do it again, and no Star (`star-round`: Explorer is not offered Stars). A link to the Sutra's deep dive (`#/sutras/s1`, `s2`, `s3`) is on every end card. A Star opened from that end card offers Back home (the modified "Back from a Star" requirement), since the Dojo's long map is not where a new Player came from.

**6. A lesson, not practice.** Each right answer earns 10 Sparks (`store.addSparks(10, 'try')`), and each answer counts the day (`store.touchDay()`) and sends `math/q_answered/try/<right|miss>`, so the first right answer is still the device's `first_win`. It does not call `store.record()`, so no Skill mastery, streak, Keep going (`S.last`), Rating, Star or Belt changes. That keeps Home's Keep going rule (`find-and-home-polish`) and the Star door exactly as a Mode card leaves them, and keeps a Try a trick from looking like progress in the Dojo. Finishing marks `try:<mode>` seen in the Player's progress (`store.markSeen`, so it travels in the backup code) and Home drops the card for that Mode.

**7. Analytics.** The analytics spec already allows a League (Mode) as a fixed word in a path (`round_end/<area>/<league>`, `landing/mode/<id>`). So each Try a trick pressed (picker or Home card) sends `math/try/start` exactly as before, then `math/try/mode/<mode>`; the route view is `math/view/try`; answers use the new `try` answer area. Nothing else is in any path or title. Rejected: `math/try/start/<mode>` (it would change the existing event's path).

**8. Accessibility, Calm and Pause motion, voice.** The start button takes focus; Enter, Space or Escape start; each question focuses its answer box (Challenger, Pro) or its heading (Explorer, then Tab to the choices); a miss moves focus to the next choice left or to Next; every result is announced once in the polite live region. Nothing moves except `fx.sparkle()` after a right answer, which is already a no-op under reduced motion, Pause motion and Calm; the steps have no entry animation; nothing times out. Voice: no new spoken line. The cards, questions and results are not read aloud in any Mode; the Sutra name plays its existing name clip only after a tap on Say it; an Explorer with Read aloud on hears only "You got it!" and "Not quite. Try again!", both already recorded (`tools/voice/lines.mjs` fixed lines). `js/try.js` has no `const PRAISE` and no other literal `speak('...')`, so the voice line scan finds nothing new and `test/voice-coverage.test.mjs` is unaffected. A spoken Explorer Try a trick is the owner's call (open items).

**9. Fit with `placement`, `age-bands`, `try-trick-teaches` and `find-and-home-polish`.**
- `placement` (not built) plans: Mode pick, then the Placement offer, then Home. Updated in its design decision 9 and task 3.1: a Mode card goes to the offer as planned; a card's Try a trick plays first and the offer replaces its end card's Back home. Decision 10 and task 4.2: its `isNewPlayer()` must not count Sparks from a Try a trick (it records no answer), so the Hook still opens the first Star from Home.
- `age-bands` (not built) will add band copy and 3 choices for Early; Explorer's copy here already keeps to short sentences and 3 choices, and a band copy table can take these lines later.
- The archive pull request (#121, branch `archive-finished-changes`) archives `try-trick-teaches`, `celebrations`, `find-and-home-polish` and `explorer-belts`. It is merged into this branch, so this change's deltas are written and validated against the specs as they read after it: `first-visit` then has "Try a trick before any choice", "One plain lede, and the Mode cards stay", "The app's hero says little", "Home fits a phone", "Keep going does not repeat the first door", "The trial Star teaches before it asks" and "The worked example card is reachable and still". This change REMOVES the first and the two trial Star ones (with `starIntro()`, `runStar({ intro })`, `starView.introFor`, their unit tests and `e2e/tests/try-trick-teaches.spec.ts`) and MODIFIES the other four for the new picker and Home path, keeping each scenario name. #121 should merge first; if it changes before it does, merge it again and re-run `openspec validate try-trick-in-modes --strict`.
- `placement`'s decision 14 and task 4.5 (added by #121 from `try-trick-teaches`'s decision 4, the card and the Hook) are marked superseded: there is no card for the Hook to meet.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Three primary buttons on the picker instead of one. Accepted: the owner asked for Try a trick inside each Mode, and a visitor reads one card at a time; each button sits with its card.
- The picker is taller on a phone (a 48 px button under each card). Mitigation: at 390 by 844 the Explorer card's Try a trick is on screen; nothing scrolls sideways at 320 px.
- Explorer kids who cannot read yet meet an unspoken card. Mitigation: the sandwich picture and the three big number buttons carry the idea; the two recorded lines are spoken. A spoken version needs a recording (open items).
- First load grows by 4,166 bytes Brotli (758,499 to 762,665, about 0.5%), almost all of it `js/try.js` (4,325 bytes; it is precached, so a lazy import would not lower the measured first load). `docs/perf/baseline.json` is raised to the new total with its file sizes, as the performance-budget spec asks of a change that grows first load on purpose.
- Players who finish Try a trick lose the Home card. The lesson stays at `#/try` (the Guide's Play modes section covers it) and the Trick's Star and page stay in the Dojo.

## Migration

None needed. A stored `modeGuess` is still read and cleared on the next pick. `try:<mode>` seen marks are new keys in the existing `seen` map.
